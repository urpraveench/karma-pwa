## Commenting on LinkedIn

**Who to follow (5 to 10 people):** people who post regularly about productivity, time management, careers, upskilling or fitness for working professionals, ideally in India, with **5,000 to 50,000 followers**. Bigger accounts bury your comment among hundreds; smaller ones may not have readers yet.

**How to find them (10 minutes, once):** on LinkedIn, search `#productivity`, then `#timemanagement`, `#upskilling`, `#careergrowth`, `#habits` and `#worklifebalance`. Tap **Posts**, filter **Date posted: Past week**, and look for people whose posts get 20 to 200 comments and whose readers are working people like your users. Follow them and tap the bell on their profile, so you see their posts as they go up. Note each one in the table below.

**How to comment:**

- **Early:** in the first hour after a post goes up, when most people read it. The bell notifications help.
- **Add something:** a number, your own experience, a question, or a polite disagreement. Two to four lines. Never just "Great post!"
- **No links, no pitch.** Never paste a TikTym link under someone else's post: it looks like spam, and LinkedIn shows such comments to fewer people. Curious readers tap your name; your profile does the rest.
- **Reply to replies.** A conversation under a popular post is seen by many more people than the comment alone.

**Example comments** (rewrite them in your own words for each post):

> On a post about morning routines: "What helped me most wasn't waking up earlier, it was making the target smaller. 30 minutes a day I actually did beat the 1 hour I kept postponing."

> On a post about streaks or consistency: "The thing that broke my habits was never the hard days, it was the day after a missed one, when the streak said zero. Letting a missed day carry forward (up to a limit) kept me going."

> On a post about upskilling while working: "Doing the maths helped me: 30 minutes on weekdays alone is about 130 hours a year. That's a whole course, without touching the weekends."

> On a post about time-blocking: "Blocking time works until meetings eat the block. What worked for me was a daily minimum instead of a fixed slot: any 30 minutes, whenever the day allows."

## Content ideas

Ideas for posts and challenges, with the plan for each. Add new ones here as they come.

### The maths of mastery: 10,000 hours (your idea, 9 Oct)

- **Hook:** "10,000 hours makes you an expert." For a working professional, 1 hour a day is 27 years; 30 minutes a day is 55. The real point: expertise comes from showing up daily for years, which is what TikTym measures.
- **Stay accurate:** the number was popularised by Malcolm Gladwell's *Outliers*, from Anders Ericsson's research. Ericsson said it's an average, not a threshold, and that deliberate practice matters more than raw hours. Say so; it makes the post more credible.
- **Close with:** "How many hours have you put into the skill you care about most? TikTym counts them for you."
- **Media:** a simple graphic of the maths (hours a day vs years to 10,000), in the brand style (ask Claude).
- **When:** post 3, around Thu 15 Oct.
- **Product idea:** hours towards mastery per practice (logged in to-do.md).

### 100 Days, 100 Minutes: consistency challenge (your idea, 9 Oct)

- **The challenge:** 100 minutes a day for 100 days: **30 Exercise + 30 Reading + 30 Upskilling + 10 Meditation**.
- **Zero setup:** these are exactly the four practices a new account starts with (Exercise, Reading, Course study, Meditation = 100 minutes). Joining is "sign up and tap Start".
- **The hook:** 100 days × 100 minutes = **10,000 minutes** (about 167 hours). "Mastery takes 10,000 hours. Start with 10,000 minutes." Pairs with the 10,000-hours post.
- **The visual:** the mala has 108 beads, one per minute, so a 100-minute day almost fills it. "Fill your mala for 100 days."
- **The safety net (and the product story):** a busy day's minutes carry forward, up to two days' worth by default, so a missed day can be made up rather than breaking the challenge. A day counts when Today's mala is cleared, carried minutes included.
- **Replaces the 30-day challenge**, with day 30 as the first checkpoint.
- **Timing:** announce once 20+ people use TikTym (week 3); start Mon 2 Nov 2026, finish Tue 9 Feb 2027.
- **Content it gives:** launch post, weekly check-ins with your progress card, milestone posts at days 30, 50 and 100, and finishers' stories.
- **Product idea:** a "Day 34 of 100" challenge view (logged in to-do.md); not needed to start.
